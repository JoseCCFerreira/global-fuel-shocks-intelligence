Global Fuel Shocks IntelligenceREADME

Fuel prices, wars, disasters, housing and population

Understand global fuel-price shocks and what moves around them.

A reproducible analytics project prepared for DuckDB, dbt, statistics, forecasting and an interactive Streamlit app.

Objective

The project studies how fuel prices vary over time, where jumps occur, and how those jumps align with wars, natural disasters, population change and house-price variation.

Data Flow

Public sourcesRaw dataProcessed tablesDuckDBdbt martsStatisticsForecastsStreamlit

What It Will Analyse

Fuel Variation

Monthly and yearly price changes, rolling volatility and long-run cycles.

Shock Detection

Z-score jump flags and event windows around abnormal movements.

Wars & Disasters

Conflict deaths and disaster damage as severity proxies for event correlation.

Housing & Population

Comparison with house-price indexes and country/world population.

Geographic Heatmap

World choropleth maps with country focus points, regional distributions and metric availability checks.

Statistics Used

Descriptive statistics, rolling averages, rolling volatility, Pearson and Spearman correlation, event-study windows, jump detection and baseline forecasting.

Decision Value

The study is designed to identify when fuel prices are moving unusually, which events coincide with those moves, and whether current conditions resemble previous shock periods.

Interactive App

The Streamlit app explores price series, shock months, country context, world heatmaps, regional catastrophe distributions, country-level variation, correlations, model metrics, feature importance and baseline forecasts.

Forecasting & Machine Learning

The machine learning layer compares a Random Forest classifier for fuel-price jump risk with a Random Forest regressor for monthly price variation. The forecast page shows a 36-month baseline projection, volatility bands and the variables that contributed most to model decisions.

Data Coverage Note

Country-level disasters, conflicts and population have usable coverage. The current World Bank gasoline and diesel pump-price extracts do not provide usable country-level values, so the app warns clearly when those metrics are selected and uses global fuel series for annual fuel-price comparison.
